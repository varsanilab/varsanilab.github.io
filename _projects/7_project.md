An investigation of enigmatic, likely-commensal anelloviruses in Delphinids (dolphins and whales)

Anellovirus infections are ubiquitous in mammals and lack any clear disease associations, suggesting a commensal virus-host relationship. Prevalence in humans approaches 90% in some populations, with initial infection occurring in a variety of tissue types shortly after birth. However, the presence and dynamics of these viruses are poorly understood among other mammalian hosts. In this project, we collaborated with subsistence farmers in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines to study the viral communities harbored by marine mammals. Our genomic approaches have informed the virus-host interactions, evolutionary dynamics, and classifications associated with Anelloviridae family members that infect Delphinids.
